CATCHMENT: Bay of Plenty-Waiariki

School roll capacity pressure in the Bay of Plenty-Waiariki region, New Zealand. Ministry of Education (MoE) data, 189 schools, 2010–2025.

At a glance

Four things the numbers below say about the region, before you dig into the charts.

  1. 1The region's roll grew 17% since 2010 (55,287 → 64,933), but that growth has flattened out: 2025 was essentially flat on 2024 (−0.1%), after a dip in 2022 and a strong 2023–24 rebound.
  2. 2Almost all of that growth is one city. Tauranga City alone added 7,233 students since 2010, about three-quarters of the region's entire net gain, while the other six TAs combined grew only slightly.
  3. 3The capacity pressure signal is rising fast: schools sitting at or above their own historical-maximum roll went from 6 in 2022 to 35 in 2025, nearly 1 in 5 schools in the region. (This is a proxy, not a measured shortage; see the note below the tables.)
  4. 4Cohort retention is mixed. Tracking the Year 5 (2020) cohort into Year 6 (2021), 64 of 151 schools (42%) show a net decline, concentrated in smaller rural and kaupapa Māori schools.

Methodology and caveats

  • Scope: region education_region = 'Bay of Plenty, Waiariki', 7 territorial authorities (Kawerau, Ōpōtiki, Rotorua, Taupo, Tauranga City, Western Bay of Plenty, Whakatane), 189 schools.
  • Roll totals come from staging_student_rolls (MoE rolls, school+year grain, 2010–2025), not fact_roll. We confirmed empirically that fact_roll's ethnicity breakdown is "total response" (a student can be counted under more than one ethnic group): summing across ethnicity overstates the real roll by roughly 2x. The one exception is cohort progression, which needs fact_roll for its year-level breakdown.
  • The capacity pressure signal is a proxy (current roll vs. a school's own historical maximum), not a real measurement of physical classroom capacity.
  • Data source: NZ Ministry of Education (Education Counts). CATCHMENT project by Gabriela Olivera. See docs/METHODOLOGY.md in the repo for full detail.
